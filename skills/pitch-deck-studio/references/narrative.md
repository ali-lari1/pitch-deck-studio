# Narrative: arcs, budgets and slide craft

A deck is an argument, not a form. Every slide either answers an investor question or removes an objection.
Treat the arcs below as starting shapes, then bend them to the company. The analysis behind them (110+
post-2022 decks) is summarised in `deck-library.md`.

## The seven questions every deck must answer
1. **What is it, and for whom?** One sentence a stranger could repeat.
2. **Why does it matter?** The pain, sized in time, money, risk or lives, and felt by a specific person.
3. **Why now?** What changed (technology, regulation, cost curve, behaviour, a platform shift) that makes this possible or urgent today.
4. **Why will it work?** Proof: traction, data, pilots, benchmarks, a demo.
5. **Why will it be big?** A bottom-up market, a business model that compounds, and room to expand.
6. **Why you?** An unfair insight, access, or track record, and why competitors can't simply copy you.
7. **What do you need, and what will it buy?** The amount, use of funds, and the milestones that de-risk the next round.

## Slide budgets (main deck, excluding appendix)
| Situation | Slides | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Demo day / 2-3 min | 6-8 | one number per slide, statement-heavy |
| Pre-seed | 8-11 | insight, team and early signal carry it |
| Seed | 10-13 | first proof plus a credible go-to-market |
| Series A | 12-15 + appendix | repeatability: cohorts, unit economics, pipeline |
| Series B+ | 14-20 + appendix | efficiency, market leadership, path to scale |
| IPO / roadshow | 30-60 incl. appendix | segments, financials, reconciliations, disclaimers |
| Send-ahead version | same count, more words in notes | cold readers spend about 2-4 minutes; slides 1-3 must carry the thesis |

## Arcs (pick one in `brief.md`, then adapt)

**A. Traction-led** (revenue is the strongest argument; B2B SaaS, fintech, marketplaces with data)
Title → *the headline metric on slide 2* → Problem → Product → How it works → Why now → Market (bottom-up) →
Business model & unit economics → Competition → Go-to-market → Team → Milestones → Ask.

**B. Insight-led** (pre-revenue, pre-seed/seed, new behaviour)
Title → Problem told through one person or one number → The insight (what you know that others don't) → Why now →
Product / demo → Early signal (waitlist, LOIs, pilots, design partners, retention of first users) → Market → Model →
Competition → Team → Ask.

**C. Demo-led** (the product itself is the wow; AI apps, dev tools, consumer)
Title → One-liner → Product in 2-3 screens or a code block → Who uses it and how much → The problem it removes →
Why now → Market → Model → Moat → Team → Ask.

**D. Science- / tech-led** (biotech, medtech, deeptech, hardware, climate)
Title → The breakthrough in one figure (data, benchmark, prototype photo) → Unmet need → Approach / platform →
Evidence (data with n, controls, error bars) → De-risking plan (TRL, clinical or regulatory path, manufacturing) →
Path to market & business model (unit-cost curve, reimbursement, offtake) → IP & moat → Team credentials →
Capital plan tied to milestones → Ask.

**E. Mission / category-creation** (defense, space, infrastructure, very large visions; modelled on the SpaceX roadshow)
Title → Mission → "N of 1" proof grid (a stats slide that sets the scale) → Why we win (an integration or cost advantage) →
Repeatable business model (flywheel) → Market built up segment by segment → Milestones delivered → Milestones ahead →
Segment deep-dives, each with section divider → Team & culture → Capital efficiency → Ask.

**F. Consumer / marketplace loop**
Title → Human insight → Product in photos and screens → The loop (how users bring users, supply brings demand) →
Traction (cohort retention, frequency, organic share) → Unit economics (CAC, contribution margin, payback) →
Market → Competition → Team → Ask.

**G. Roadshow / IPO / late-stage** (from public roadshow decks)
Title → Disclaimer / forward-looking statements → Offering summary → Mission → Platform overview → Key stats grid →
Investment highlights ("why we win") → Business model → Market opportunity → Recent milestones → Segment sections
(each with KPIs and charts) → Growth strategy → Capital allocation track record → Financial overview & targets →
Appendix: revenue components, key metric definitions, non-GAAP reconciliations. Label this "Not legal advice" and
have counsel review the disclaimers.

### Bending rules
- **Strongest card first.** Whatever the best proof is (revenue, a famous customer, a founder exit, a benchmark),
  put it in the first three slides. A cold reader may give up after slide 3.
- **Team early** when the team *is* the reason (repeat founders, rare credentials, deep tech), and **late** otherwise.
- **Why now** belongs next to the problem when the timing argument is non-obvious, and is cut when it is obvious.
- **Competition** is required at seed and later. Do it as a positioning matrix or an honest comparison table, never "no competitors".
- **Market** is bottom-up (units × price × frequency) with the arithmetic visible. Top-down TAM circles, if used, back it up and do not replace it.
- **Cut** any slide that doesn't change the investor's mind. Move detail to the appendix or the notes.

## Slide craft

| Slide | What good looks like | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Title | name, one-line value prop, round & date; brand-forward, nothing else | tagline with buzzwords |
| Problem | one person, one quantified pain, ideally one striking stat with source | three generic bullets |
| Insight / why now | the specific change (cost fell 90%, regulation X, new behaviour), dated | "AI is changing everything" |
| Product | real screenshots in frames, a code sample, a hardware photo; captions say the benefit | feature lists |
| How it works | 3-4 steps, verbs, a time or cost per step | architecture diagram for a business audience |
| Traction | one chart, headline states the rate, a source line, highlighted latest point | vanity metrics, cumulative charts hiding a flat trend |
| Unit economics | CAC, payback, gross margin, retention, in a row grid; definitions in notes | blended numbers without definitions |
| Market | bottom-up math shown; beachhead → expansion | $100B TAM from a report title |
| Business model | who pays, how much, how often, margin | "multiple revenue streams" |
| Competition | 2×2 on axes that matter to the buyer, or a table with honest partial marks | every row green for you |
| Go-to-market | channel, cost and proof it works (first 10 customers came from...) | "viral + partnerships + SEO" |
| Team | photos, name, role, the one credential that matters; logos of past companies sparingly | full CVs |
| Milestones | dated, measurable, tied to the money | vague phases |
| Ask | amount, instrument, use of funds as % with what each buys, runway, the milestone it reaches | "raising $X" alone |
| Closing | name, one-line mission, contact | "Questions?" |
| Appendix | cohort tables, financial model summary, detailed competition, technical deep-dive, FAQ | main-deck slides dumped |

## The headline test and copy rules
- List all headlines in order. Read them aloud. They must form a coherent paragraph that makes the case. If one is a label
  ("Market", "Team"), rewrite it as a claim ("A $1.9B wedge inside a $9B market").
- At most 12 words per headline. Give each slide one accent-highlighted phrase (`==...==`), at most.
- Use numbers rather than adjectives. Cut "revolutionary", "disruptive", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "world-class",
  "leverage", "synergy", "game-changing", "unique", "next-generation", "AI-powered" (unless the AI is the point and you say what it does).
- Specific beats general: "pharmacists in rural Ohio" beats "healthcare professionals".
- Every chart has a title that states the conclusion, a source line, and one highlighted data point.
- No claim without evidence. "Best", "leading" and "only" need a comparison or are removed.
- Be consistent: the same metric name and definition everywhere (ARR ≠ run-rate ≠ bookings).
- Notes carry the reasoning. Slides carry the conclusion.

## Presented vs send-ahead
Build one `deck.md`. Slides stay sparse (target ≤ 40 words per slide on average). Notes under `???` hold the
voice-over and are 2-5 sentences, written so a reader without the founder understands the slide. `deck.html?reader`
renders the send-ahead version (slide plus notes). For a PDF-only send-ahead, raise density a little on the
problem, product and traction slides (a short paragraph under the headline is fine).
