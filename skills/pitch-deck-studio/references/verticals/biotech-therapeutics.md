- Archetypes: company-creation firms (Flagship, Atlas, ARCH, Third Rock, Versant); specialist crossover/growth (RA Capital, OrbiMed, Foresite, Frazier, F-Prime, Sofinnova, 5AM, DCVC Bio); pharma CVCs (Lilly, Novartis, J&J, Pfizer ventures); sovereign/Asia (Ally Bridge, Alpha Wave); non-dilutive (NIH, ARPA-H, CEPI, foundations).

## 2. What they scrutinize first
1. The asset, not the platform: specific drug, disease, path to human data ("the money wants an asset") ([WeWillCure](https://wewillcure.com/insights/news/funding-and-models/vcs-writing-biotech-series-a-checks-in-2026)).
2. Target/biology validation and differentiated data (animal efficacy, translational biomarkers, human genetics).
3. Regulatory/clinical path and cost to next value inflection: IND-enabling package, FDA Q-sub, Phase 1 design/readout timing.
4. IP: composition-of-matter, freedom to operate, licensing terms (academic/pharma).
5. Team and syndicate; exit logic (M&A windows open: Lilly/Kelonia $7B, [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/pitchbook-q2-2026-megadeals-87-percent-us-venture-vintage-discount)).

## 4. Slide emphasis
- Ideal: 20-30 (scientific decks run long; keep main <=20, appendix for data).
- Promote: **target & mechanism**, **data package** (in vitro/in vivo, dose-response, n, statistics), **development plan with milestones and cash**, **IP**, **team & SAB**.
- Add: **regulatory path**, **clinical trial design & endpoints**, **competitive landscape (pipeline table by stage)**, **commercial/pricing & reimbursement**, **partnering/exit logic**.
- Demote: generic TAM; platform hype; long background science.

## 5. Proof devices
Data figures with error bars and n; head-to-head vs standard of care or competitor; KOL quotes and SAB; FDA meeting minutes (pre-IND/Q-sub); development timeline Gantt with funded milestones; patent figure and claim summary; pharma term-sheet/option interest.

## 6. Design and tone
Data-dense, scientific credibility: restrained palette, publication-quality plots, citations on-slide (PMIDs), consistent units. Calm and precise; do not oversell; label preliminary results.

## 7. Red flags
Platform without lead asset; single-animal-study efficacy; no clear IND path; weak IP or unlicensed academic IP; unrealistic timelines; thin team vs asset complexity; cash that doesn't reach a value inflection; crowded target without differentiation; stale data.

## 8. 2025-26 context
- Seed/Series A financing lagged the broader recovery: $4.4B in H1 2026 (-15%) with record-low round counts ([BioCentury](https://www.biocentury.com/article/660172/seed-and-series-a-financing-lagged-biotech-s-broader-recovery-in-1h26)); first financings on pace for lowest annual count since pre-COVID (JPM Q1 2026).
- Bifurcated: fewer companies funded, high-quality assets get multiple sources; open IPO window, reverse mergers and elevated M&A in Q3 2026 ([JPM Q3](https://www.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpmorgan/documents/cb/insights/outlook/jpm-biopharma-deck-q3-2026.pdf)).
- Large, asset-led Series A rounds ($80-300M) replace platform-led small rounds.
