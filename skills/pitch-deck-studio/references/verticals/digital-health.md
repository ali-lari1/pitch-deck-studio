## 1. Who funds it now
- Archetypes: health-specialist VCs (Rock Health, Define Ventures, Oak HC/FT, Optum Ventures, Town Hall, Frist Cressey Ventures, Flare Capital, Bessemer Health); big platforms (General Catalyst/HATCo, a16z Bio+Health, Lightspeed, Khosla, Thrive, Andreessen); health-system and payer CVCs (Kaiser/Permanente Ventures, Mayo, Cleveland Clinic, UnitedHealth/Optum); AI crossover funds. Names unlinked are background; verify.
- Evidence: Lightspeed led Mati Health's $19M Series A (2026, [mean.ceo roundup](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=11233)); Techstars runs healthcare accelerators with Permanente Medicine ([listing](https://www.heysuccess.com/opportunity/Techstars-Healthcare-Accelerator-powered-by-Permanente-Medicine-Mid-Atlantic-States-52660)).

## 2. What they scrutinize first
1. Who pays: payer, employer, health system, or consumer; reimbursement codes and contracts, not subscriptions ([Seedtable](https://www.seedtable.com/best-digital-health-startups)).
2. Clinical/outcomes evidence and ROI: measurable cost offset, peer-reviewed or payer-validated.
3. Regulatory path: FDA (SaMD) or not, HIPAA, state licensing, clinical-AI liability.
4. Sales cycle and pilot conversion with health systems/payers (long, 6-18 months).
5. Workflow fit and clinician adoption; AI scribe/agent crowding.

## 4. Slide emphasis
- Ideal: 12-16.
- Promote: **buyer/payer map and reimbursement**, outcomes evidence, workflow integration (EHR), pilot pipeline.
- Add: **regulatory path** (FDA/HIPAA/state), **clinical validation**, **implementation timeline**, **safety & AI governance**, **channel/sales cycle**.
- Demote: consumer-style growth hacks, generic "healthcare is broken" stats, long TAM.

## 5. Proof devices
Named health-system/payer pilots with outcome numbers; peer-reviewed or IRB-backed results; clinician testimonials; EHR integration screenshots; savings per member per month; HIPAA/SOC 2/HITRUST badges.

## 6. Design and tone
Credible and calm: clean, accessible, clinical-adjacent visuals; real patient/clinician context (consented); numeric evidence over adjectives; compliance iconography visible but not dominant.

## 7. Red flags
Consumer-pay model without retention; pilots that never convert; no named economic buyer; unproven clinical claims; unclear FDA posture for AI; heavy services/human cost; dependence on one payer.

## 8. 2025-26 context
- H1 2026 US digital health $7.4B/244 deals (vs $6.4B H1 2025); mega-deals took 45% of dollars; median deal $14M ([Fierce](https://fiercehealthcare.com/digital-health/digital-health-brought-74b-vc-funding-ai-powered-rebound-fuels-market), [MarketScale](https://www.marketscale.com/industries/healthcare/rock-health-74b-went-into-digital-health-in-h1-2026-and-20-rounds-took-45.md)).
