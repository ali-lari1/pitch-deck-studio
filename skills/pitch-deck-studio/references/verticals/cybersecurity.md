## 1. Who funds it now
- Archetypes: security specialists; Israeli ecosystem funds; multi-stage generalists; strategics/CVCs (Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, Google etc.); acquirer-aware late-stage.
- Evidence: YL Ventures (seed-only cyber, >$800M raised over five funds), Ballistic Ventures (seed-B, $360M Fund II, led a $31M Series A for Native in 2026), Cyberstarts (pre-product seed, CISO network; Wiz, Cyera, Island) ([Waveup list](https://waveup.com/blog/top-cybersecurity-venture-capital-firms/), [Betaboom](https://www.betaboom.com/magazine/article/seed-stage-cybersecurity-vcs-investing), [Gracker](https://gracker.ai/cyber-resources/vcs/cyberstarts), vendor/directory pages). Background (verify): Team8, Evolution Equity, Forgepoint, Sequoia, Index, Greylock, Lightspeed, Acrew.
- AI security seed: >$855M across 150+ seed rounds in 2026 (Oak $60M, Cylake $45M seed, JetStream $34M seed) ([AI Weekly citing Crunchbase](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/ai-security-startups-pull-855m-across-150-seed-rounds-in-2026)).

## 2. What they scrutinize first
1. Is the pain budgeted (named budget owner, CISO design partners) and why now (AI agents, identity, regulation)?
2. Differentiation vs platform vendors (will CrowdStrike/Palo Alto/Microsoft bundle this?), and exit logic (strategic acquirers).
3. Technical depth and founder credibility (research, prior exits, nation-state/ops background).
4. Land-expand motion, sales cycle, POV-to-contract conversion; false-positive rate and deployment friction.
5. Trust posture: SOC 2/ISO, data handling, your own security.

## 4. Slide emphasis
- Ideal: 12-14.
- Promote: threat/why now, product architecture diagram, design-partner list, detection efficacy numbers, integration with SIEM/EDR/IdP.
- Add: **platform-risk/competitive map**, **security posture & compliance**, **buyer persona/budget**, **acquirer map**.
- Demote: generic breach statistics, vendor-fear imagery.

## 5. Proof devices
Red-team results; third-party evaluation (MITRE ATT&CK-style, independent pen-test); time-to-detect/response numbers; CVE counts found; design partner quotes (CISO names with permission); live demo of attack and remediation; deployment time in hours.

## 6. Design and tone
Sober, technical, high-trust. Dark UI, architecture diagrams, threat-model visuals; avoid FUD. Precise numbers with methods.

## 7. Red flags
Feature, not a product (bundled by platforms); high false positives; long deploy; no champion budget; founders without security credibility; claims of "AI-powered" without detail; own security failures.
