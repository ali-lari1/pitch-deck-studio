## 2. What they scrutinize first
1. Regulatory model: licenses vs bank partner; sponsor-bank risk after the Synapse collapse (froze ~$265M of customer funds, per a startup memo; verify) ([techhub memo](https://techhub.it.com/investors), unverified).
2. Unit economics: take rate / net revenue per $ volume, loss rate, CAC payback, contribution margin; interchange or NIM sensitivity.
3. Distribution advantage: embedded/B2B channel vs paid consumer acquisition.
4. Risk and fraud model; credit performance by vintage; capital structure (warehouse lines).
5. AI/stablecoin angle: infrastructure and AI-native products attract capital; consumer apps are starved ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/fintech-funding-surges-23-in-h1-2026-driven-by-ai-and-infrastructure-bets), commentary).

## 4. Slide emphasis
- Ideal: 12-15.
- Promote: regulatory/licensing structure slide, unit economics per $ of volume, credit/fraud performance, partnerships (bank, processors, distribution).
- Add: **compliance & risk architecture**, **cohort loss curves**, **capital/funding stack**, **customer concentration by channel**.
- Demote: vision of "banking reimagined", UI-only decks without economics.

## 5. Proof devices
TPV/volume chart; take-rate waterfall; vintage loss curves; bank/regulator letters or licence numbers; audited controls (SOC 2, PCI DSS); live transaction demo; named design partner or embedded distribution partner with volume.

## 6. Design and tone
Trustworthy: restrained palette, precise numbers, risk and compliance up-front. Consumer fintech: brand-forward but proof-heavy. B2B/infrastructure: architecture diagrams.

## 7. Red flags
Dependence on one sponsor bank; unclear regulatory perimeter; paid-acquisition-only growth with CAC > lifetime margin; undefined loss reserves; round-tripping volume; stablecoin products without compliance plan; high concentration in one merchant.
