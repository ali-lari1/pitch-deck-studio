# Developer tools / open source

Legend: linked = 2026 sweep; unlinked names = background, verify; **[pre-2024]** = older benchmark.

## 1. Who funds it now
- Archetypes: developer-specialist funds (Heavybit, Boldstart, Amplify, Decibel, Essence, OSS Capital); multi-stage platforms (Sequoia, a16z, Accel, Index, Redpoint, Insight, Bessemer, Lightspeed); YC (large share of dev-tool and open-source batches, ~150 YC open-source companies listed in 2026, [YC directory](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/industry/open-source)); strategics (GitHub/Microsoft, Datadog, Vercel-style ecosystem angels); AI crossover funds for AI-coding tools.
- Evidence: Cursor Series D $2.3B at $29.3B (Nov 2025) and Grafana's ~$270M at >$6B (Aug 2024), via a blog citing Crunchbase ([mean.ceo](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=5737)). Bessemer, Lux, Founders Fund, Lightspeed among top unicorn-cohort investors ([Pipeline Road](https://pipelineroad.com/news/20260819-sequoia-khosla-y-combinator-lead-2026-unicorn-investors)).

## 2. What they scrutinize first
1. Adoption that is organic: active users/contributors, weekly installs/downloads, retention of developers (not star count).
2. Open-source to revenue path: what is paid (hosting, enterprise features, support), conversion rate, license risk (cloud-provider cloning).
3. Bottom-up to top-down motion: self-serve to team to enterprise; time from first commit to procurement.
4. AI-coding disruption: does the tool matter when agents write code; testing/review/observability for AI code is the growth area ([Seedtable](https://www.seedtable.com/startups-developer-tools)).
5. Founder credibility with developers (maintainer track record).

## 4. Slide emphasis
- Ideal: 10-12.
- Promote: product demo/code (slide 2-3), adoption graph (downloads/WAU), community health (contributors, issues closed), pricing and open-core boundary.
- Add: **OSS strategy/license slide**, **developer love quotes**, **integration/ecosystem map**, **security & supply-chain posture**.
- Demote: enterprise logos before usage proof; long market sizing.

## 5. Proof devices
Terminal/code snippets that run; GitHub star/contributor charts (annotated, with WAU next to it); npm/PyPI/Docker pulls; "time to first value" screencast; benchmarks vs incumbents with reproducible repo; maintainer testimonials; public changelog velocity.

## 6. Design and tone
Developer-native: dark mode, monospace accents, real product/CLI screenshots, GitHub badges. Technical honesty, no marketing language; show the repo link on slide 1.

## 7. Red flags
Star-farming; users that never convert; license that invites cloud clones; no differentiation vs AI-coding agents; heavy dependence on one platform API; services-heavy revenue; founder is not a maintainer.

## 8. 2025-26 context
- AI-assisted coding changed demand: testing, code review, observability and AI-agent infrastructure are hot; consolidation pressure on point tools ([Seedtable](https://www.seedtable.com/startups-developer-tools)).
- AI-coding mega-valuations (Cursor $29.3B) pull attention and capital to the category ([mean.ceo/Crunchbase](https://blog.mean.ceo/?p=5737)).
- Bottom-up adoption remains dominant distribution (Seedtable).
